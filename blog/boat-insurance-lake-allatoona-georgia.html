Why boat insurance matters at Lake Allatoona

Your homeowner's insurance won't cover your boat. That's the first thing people are surprised to learn. If something happens to your boat, your house policy is basically useless. You could be looking at tens of thousands of dollars in repairs or replacement costs with nothing to back you up.

Lake Allatoona sees decent boat traffic, especially on weekends and holidays. More boats on the water means more chances for accidents, collisions, or just plain bad luck. I've seen situations where someone's boat got damaged in a storm, or there was a collision with another vessel, and the owner had no coverage. It's brutal.

Beyond damage to your own boat, there's liability. If you damage someone else's boat or hurt someone while you're out on the water, you could be personally responsible for those costs. That's where liability coverage comes in. It protects you if you're found at fault for property damage or injuries to other people.

What your boat insurance should actually cover

Most boat policies break down into a few main areas. Collision coverage handles damage to your boat if you hit something, whether that's another boat, the dock, or rocks. Comprehensive coverage takes care of things you can't control, like theft, weather damage, or vandalism. A lot of boat owners around Lake Allatoona deal with summer storms, so comprehensive is worth serious consideration.

Then you've got liability, which I mentioned. This is what covers other people's property and medical bills if something's your fault. Medical payments coverage is another piece that covers injuries to you or your passengers, regardless of who's at fault. It's peace of mind when people are climbing on and off your boat.

Uninsured boater coverage is something people don't always think about, but it matters. If some other boat hits you and that person doesn't have insurance, you're protected instead of being stuck holding the bag.

Finding the right rate on your boat insurance

Here's where a lot of people go wrong. They call one or two insurance companies, get a quote, and figure that's their best option. In reality, boat insurance rates vary wildly between carriers. A policy that costs $800 with one company might be $1,200 with another for the exact same coverage.

At The East Agency here in Cartersville, we shop around with more than 20 different carriers to find you the best rate. We're not locked into one company or a handful of them. The other thing that affects your rate is how you use your boat. How many days a year are you actually taking it out? Do you trailer it or keep it in the water year-round? Are you using it for fishing only, or are you towing skiers? Do you have safety training? All of that stuff matters and changes your premium.
