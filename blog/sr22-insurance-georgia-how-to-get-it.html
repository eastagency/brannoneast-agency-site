SR-22 Insurance Georgia: How to Get It (Step by Step)

October 12, 2026 · By Brannon East

What is SR-22 insurance and why Georgia requires it

An SR-22 is not actually an insurance policy—it's a certificate of financial responsibility that you file with the Georgia Department of Driver Services. Basically, it's proof that you have the minimum liability insurance Georgia requires. You'll need one if you've been convicted of certain driving violations: DUI or DWI, reckless driving, driving with a suspended or revoked license, or accumulating too many points on your license in a short time.

When the state requires an SR-22, your insurance company files it electronically on your behalf. If you let your policy lapse or cancel it without replacing it, your insurer has to notify the DDS. That typically means your license gets suspended again, and you're looking at an even bigger headache.

How to get SR-22 insurance in Georgia

The process is pretty straightforward, but it does matter who you work with. First, you need an actual auto insurance policy. You can't get an SR-22 without insurance. Once you have a policy in place, your agent contacts your state and files the SR-22 certificate. Most of the time, it goes through the same day or within a business day.

Here's the part that trips people up: not every insurance company will write policies for drivers who need an SR-22. Some carriers avoid high-risk drivers altogether. That's where shopping around really pays off. We work with over 20 different carriers here at The East Agency, which means we can find companies willing to insure you even when you're in this situation. In Cartersville and across Bartow County, plenty of people have landed their license suspension, and we know exactly which insurers will actually work with them.

What to expect with rates and filing fees

SR-22 insurance does cost more than standard auto insurance. You're typically looking at 50 to 100 percent higher premiums, depending on what your violation was and your overall driving history. On top of your insurance premium, Georgia charges a $20 SR-22 filing fee each time it's filed or renewed.

The good news is that the higher rate isn't permanent. Most SR-22 requirements last three years in Georgia. Once that period ends and you maintain a clean driving record, your rates will drop back to normal levels. Some people see improvement even sooner if their original violation was isolated and everything else looks clean.

How long you'll need to keep it

The state determines how long you need to carry an SR-22. For most violations, it's three years from the date of the incident or the date you reinstate your license, whichever is later. If your violation was a DUI, it could be longer. Your insurance agent—or the court paperwork you received—will tell you exactly when the requirement ends.

Do not cancel your policy early or let it lapse thinking you're done. The SR-22 requirement stays active for the full period, and if your coverage lapses, the state gets notified automatically. You'll face penalties, and your license can get suspended again.
