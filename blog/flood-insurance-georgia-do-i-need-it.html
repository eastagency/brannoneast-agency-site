Do I need flood insurance in Georgia? Here's what to know

I get this question a lot, and honestly, it's one of the most important ones you can ask. Most people assume their homeowners insurance covers flood damage. It doesn't. Not even close. And if you live in Georgia, whether you're in Cartersville, Bartow County, or anywhere else in the state, you need to understand whether flood insurance makes sense for your situation.

Here's the thing: standard homeowners policies are designed to cover wind, fire, theft, and some weather events. Flooding is specifically excluded. I've seen families go through major financial hardship because they didn't realize this until water was in their living room. That's why I'm writing this today.

What counts as flood damage in Georgia

First, let's be clear about what we're talking about. Flood insurance covers damage from overflow of inland or tidal waters, unusual and rapid accumulation of runoff, mudslides triggered by flooding, and water that enters your home from saturated ground. It doesn't cover burst pipes, water heater leaks, or water backup from your plumbing. Those fall under your homeowners policy.

Georgia gets heavy rain, and we're not immune to tropical systems either. The state experiences flooding from riverine sources, localized heavy rainfall, and coastal storm surge depending on where you are. In recent years, communities across Georgia have dealt with serious flood events that left people scrambling.

Do you actually need it? Ask yourself these questions

You're in a higher-risk zone if you live in a designated flood zone, even if you're just in the fringe area. Your mortgage lender will require flood insurance if your property is in a high-risk FEMA flood zone. That's non-negotiable. If you're outside those zones but own your home outright, the decision is yours to make.

But here's what I tell people: check your area. Do you live near a river, stream, or creek? Has your property flooded before? Do you live in a low-lying area where water tends to collect during heavy rain? Are you in a flood zone according to FEMA's flood maps? Even if you're not, about 20 percent of flood claims come from outside the highest-risk zones. That matters.

Consider your replacement cost too. If you lost everything on your first floor, how would you rebuild? Most homeowners can't absorb that kind of loss without insurance.

Your options and what they cost

Flood insurance comes from the National Flood Insurance Program, which is administered through private carriers. Rates vary based on your flood risk zone, the age of your home, its elevation relative to the base flood elevation, and the coverage amount you choose. Premium costs typically run anywhere from $400 to $3,000 a year, depending on those factors.

The peace of mind angle

I won't pretend flood insurance is fun to buy. Nobody gets excited about it. But I've also seen what happens when it's not there. Families end up paying out of pocket for tens of thousands in damage. They lose belongings they can't replace. They move away from their communities because they can't recover.

For a few hundred dollars a year, you can avoid all that. And in Georgia, where weather patterns seem to be shifting and heavy rain events are becoming more common, that protection feels less optional every year.
