September 21, 2026 · By Brannon East

RV insurance Georgia: Full timer and seasonal coverage explained

If you're hitting the road full-time in your RV or pulling it out for weekend adventures across Georgia, you need the right insurance. The thing is, RV coverage isn't one-size-fits-all. Whether you're a full timer living permanently in your rig or a seasonal traveler, the coverage you need is different. And honestly, a lot of people get this wrong because they don't know the options that actually exist.

Here at The East Agency in Cartersville, we've helped plenty of RV owners figure out what makes sense for their situation. We work with 20+ insurance carriers, so we can actually find you real options instead of just pushing you toward whatever's easy for us to sell. That matters when you're talking about protecting your home on wheels.

Full timer RV insurance is its own thing

If your RV is your permanent residence, standard auto insurance is not going to cut it. Full-time RV insurance recognizes that you're living in that vehicle year-round. That means coverage for your personal belongings inside, liability protection that accounts for the fact that people are actually living there, and usually some additional protections that part-time policies skip.

The carriers we work with understand this. They know you need coverage for your slideouts, your awning, your attached equipment. They know you're storing your life in there. Full timer policies typically include coverage for attached accessories and personal property in a way that makes sense for someone whose RV is genuinely home.

One thing people sometimes don't realize: if you're a full timer and claim residency in your RV in Georgia, you'll want to make sure your policy reflects that. Some carriers have specific full-time RV policies. Others use what they call a motorhome policy with full-timer riders. The details matter, and that's exactly why shopping with an agent beats trying to figure this out online.

Seasonal RV coverage is lighter and usually cheaper

If you're using your RV seasonally, maybe pulling your travel trailer out for summer camping or taking your motorhome to Florida for the winters, your insurance needs are genuinely different. Seasonal RV insurance is designed for people who use their rig part of the year and park it or store it the rest of the time.

Here's what makes this work financially: you don't pay for coverage when you're not using the vehicle. Some policies let you pause or reduce your coverage during off-season months. That's a real savings if you're not on the road. The trade-off is that you need to be honest about when and how you're using it. If you say it's seasonal and then live in it year-round, that's a problem when you file a claim.

Seasonal users in our area, whether you're heading out from Bartow County or elsewhere in Georgia, typically want coverage that still protects them adequately while they're traveling but doesn't waste money on months when the RV is sitting still.

Coverage limits and deductibles you actually need

Both full-time and seasonal policies need liability coverage. If someone gets hurt in or around your RV, you're on the hook. Most states, including Georgia, have minimum liability requirements. But minimum isn't smart. We usually recommend higher limits because an accident with property damage or injury can cost way more than people think.

Comprehensive and collision coverage is optional, but if you're financing or leasing your RV, your lender will require it. If you own it outright, it's your call, but most people keep it. The deductible you choose affects your premium. Pick something you could actually pay if you had to file a claim, not just the cheapest option in the moment.

Personal property coverage is worth paying attention to. If you're a full timer, that's where you're protecting all your stuff inside the RV. If you're seasonal, you might not need as much, but think about what's actually in there when you're using it.

How often you travel, where you travel, what you're towing or carrying, whether you're full-time or seasonal, whether you have a mortgage on your place (yes, that matters for some policies), what the RV is worth. All of that changes what the right answer is.
