Car Insurance After Accident Georgia: Will Your Rates Go Up?

By Brannon East · November 2, 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

So you've been in an accident. Maybe it was your fault, maybe it wasn't. Either way, you're probably wondering what comes next with your insurance rates. The truth is, car insurance after accident Georgia claims almost always means a rate increase, but there's more to the story than that. And yes, there are actually some options worth exploring.

I've had plenty of folks come into The East Agency here in Cartersville asking this exact question. They're worried, they're frustrated, and they want to know how bad it's going to get. Let me walk you through what really happens and what you can actually do about it.

Will your rates go up after an accident in Georgia?

The short answer: yes, in most cases. An at-fault accident is basically a red flag to insurance companies. They see it as a signal that you might be a higher risk, so they adjust your premiums to match that perceived risk. How much they go up depends on several things: the severity of the accident, whether anyone was injured, the amount of damage, and what your driving record looked like before the accident happened.

Georgia doesn't have a specific law capping how much insurers can raise rates after an accident, which means each company has some flexibility in how they handle it. On average, you might see an increase anywhere from 15 to 40 percent, though I've seen cases go higher depending on the circumstances. But here's the thing that a lot of people don't realize: your specific insurance company matters a lot. Some carriers are more forgiving about accidents than others.

How long does an accident stay on your record?

Most insurers in Georgia will look at accidents for three to five years, though some look further back. This doesn't mean your rates stay elevated that whole time. Usually the biggest hit happens right after the accident and at your next renewal. Then it tends to improve gradually as time passes, especially if you stay accident-free going forward.

What a lot of people don't know is that you have options. You're not locked in with one company forever, and you're not stuck paying whatever rate they throw at you. This is actually where shopping around becomes really important.

What you can actually do about it

First, ask your current insurance company about accident forgiveness. Some policies include this as an optional add-on, and if you have it, your first accident might not trigger a rate increase at all. It's worth checking your policy.

Second, consider switching companies. I know that sounds obvious, but a lot of people don't realize how differently insurance carriers treat accidents. One company might bump you up 35 percent while another might only go up 20 percent for the exact same accident. The difference could be hundreds of dollars a year.

This is exactly why we shop 20+ carriers at The East Agency. When someone comes in after an accident, we don't just accept the rate their current company gave them. We run quotes across multiple insurers to find out who's going to treat them fairly. We've found that different companies have completely different appetites for risk, and what's expensive with one carrier might be reasonable with another.

Third, look for discounts you might be missing. After an accident, people sometimes forget to ask about things like defensive driving course discounts or bundling discounts. Taking a defensive driving course in Georgia can actually lower your rates, and it shows insurance companies you're taking safety seriously.

A few other things worth knowing

If the accident wasn't your fault, push back on your current company. Make sure they're documenting it as not-at-fault. That distinction matters for rate purposes. Also, your deductible choice plays into this. Sometimes adjusting your deductible can offset some of the rate increase you're facing.

For folks here in Cartersville and Bartow County, we see a lot of accidents during those rainy stretches in spring and summer. Weather-related accidents sometimes get treated differently than others, so that's worth mentioning to your agent.

The bottom line: yes, your rates will probably go up after an accident in Georgia. But how much they go up and for how long depends partly on your insurance company's philosophy and partly on what you do next. You have more control over this situation than you might think right now.
