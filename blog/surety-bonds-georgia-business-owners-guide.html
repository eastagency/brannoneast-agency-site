Do You Need a Surety Bond in Georgia? A Guide for Business Owners

By Brannon East · November 3, 2026 · 6 min read
← Back to Blog

A good chunk of the calls coming into The East Agency lately aren't about insurance at all. They're about bonds. "Do I need to be bonded?" "What's the difference between a bond and insurance?" "Someone told me I can't get my license without one." If any of that sounds familiar, you're not alone. It's simpler than it sounds once somebody walks you through it.

I added surety bonds alongside our regular insurance lines because so many of our Georgia business clients needed both anyway. A contractor who needs a license bond almost always needs general liability too. A convenience store owner getting bonded to sell lottery tickets usually needs a Business Owners Policy on the same call. So here's what you actually need to know.

A bond is not insurance

Insurance protects you from your own losses. A surety bond protects someone else, the city, state agency, court, or business that's requiring it, from losses you might cause. If you fail to do what the bond guarantees, the obligee (whoever required the bond) can file a claim, the surety company pays it, and then you're on the hook to pay the surety back. That's the whole reason bond pricing leans so heavily on your personal credit instead of a typical insurance risk pool. The surety isn't expecting a few claims here and there. It's expecting none.

Who actually needs one in Georgia

More people than you'd think. Contractors come up a lot. Certain Georgia license classes require a bond as an alternative to meeting a net worth threshold, and public construction projects often need bid, performance, and payment bonds together (we broke that down on our Contract & Construction Bonds page).

Convenience store and retail owners are another big one, and honestly this is the group that gets caught off guard most often. If you want to sell Georgia Lottery tickets, the Georgia Lottery Corporation can require a surety bond sized to your store's sales volume before they'll issue your retailer license. Nobody thinks about this until they're already mid-application. I wrote up the specifics on our Georgia Lottery Retailer Bond page if that's you.

Notaries need one too. Your Georgia notary commission doesn't actually become active until your bond is filed with the Clerk of Superior Court in your county. Auto dealers are required to carry one if you're licensed to sell new or used motor vehicles in Georgia. And if you've been named executor, administrator, or guardian for someone's estate or a loved one's affairs, Georgia probate court often requires a fiduciary bond unless the will waives it (more on that on our Court & Fiduciary Bonds page).

There are quite a few less common ones too, vehicle title bonds, tax bonds, utility bonds, all covered on our Commercial Bonds page if you're not sure which bucket you fall into.

One thing we don't do: bail bonds. If that's what you're looking for, this isn't the right place. Everything above is business, license, and court bonds only.

How much does a surety bond actually cost

Most bonds run somewhere between 0.5% and 10% of the full bond amount per year, and where you land in that range comes down almost entirely to your personal credit. Strong credit usually means a rate near the bottom. Limited or rocky credit pushes it higher, and a handful of bond types just charge a flat premium no matter what. The bond amount itself isn't something we guess at. It's set by whoever is requiring the bond, so step one is always confirming that exact figure before we quote anything.

How the process actually works

Tell us what you need bonded for and who's requiring it. If you don't know the exact bond type or amount, that's fine, we'll confirm it with the obligee directly instead of making you track it down. From there we shop multiple surety markets to find your best rate and walk you through the application. Most standard bonds get approved within a day or two. Larger or more complicated ones take a bit longer, but you'll know what to expect going in.
