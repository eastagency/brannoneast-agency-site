Selling Lottery Tickets in Georgia Requires a Bond

If you own a convenience store, gas station, or retail location in Georgia and want to sell Georgia Lottery tickets, the Georgia Lottery Corporation (GLC) can require you to post a surety bond before issuing your retailer license. This requirement comes from Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 50-27-19), which gives the Lottery Corporation authority to require a bond sized to your store's lottery ticket sales.

The exact bond amount is not a flat fee. The Lottery Corporation sets it based on your projected or historical weekly ticket sales, so two stores can have very different requirements. Brannon confirms your specific amount directly rather than quoting a generic number, then shops the surety market to get you bonded at the best rate for your credit profile.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a bond to sell Georgia Lottery tickets?

The Georgia Lottery Corporation can require a surety bond as a condition of your retailer license under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. 50-27-19). Whether a bond is required and the exact amount depends on your projected or historical ticket sales and is determined by the Lottery Corporation when you apply.

How much is a Georgia Lottery retailer bond?

The bond amount is set by the Georgia Lottery Corporation based on your store's sales volume, not a flat statewide fee. Brannon will confirm your exact required amount with the Lottery Corporation and get you an accurate quote, instead of guessing based on a number you saw online.

Can I bundle my lottery bond with my convenience store insurance?

Yes. Most convenience store owners need general liability, a Business Owners Policy, and sometimes workers compensation in addition to their lottery bond. Brannon handles both in one conversation so you are not juggling separate agents for your bond and your store coverage.

How long does it take to get bonded?

Once Brannon has your application information and the exact bond amount confirmed with the Lottery Corporation, most retailer bonds can be issued within a few business days.

Does it cost more to use an independent agent instead of buying direct?

No. Surety companies charge the same premium whether you buy directly or through an agent like Brannon. The surety pays the agent's commission out of their own overhead, not by charging you extra. Brannon shops multiple surety markets on your behalf to find your best rate.
