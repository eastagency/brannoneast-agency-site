Term vs. Permanent Life Insurance

Term life insurance provides a death benefit for a specific period — typically 10, 20, or 30 years — at the lowest possible premium. It's ideal for income replacement during your working years or to cover a mortgage. Permanent life insurance (whole life, universal life, or indexed universal life) builds cash value over time and provides lifelong protection. The right choice depends on your age, health, budget, and long-term goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance do I actually need?

A common starting point is 10 to 12 times your annual income, but the right amount depends on your debts, dependents, mortgage balance, and income replacement goals. Brannon will walk you through a simple needs analysis at no charge.

What is the difference between term and whole life insurance?

Term life covers you for a set period, such as 10, 20, or 30 years, and pays a death benefit if you pass during that term. It is affordable and straightforward. Whole life covers you permanently and builds cash value over time. The right choice depends on your goals and budget.

Can I get life insurance if I have health issues?

Often yes. Many carriers cover people with managed conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure. Rates vary by carrier and condition. As an independent agent, Brannon shops multiple carriers to find the best fit for your health profile.

What is the average life insurance cost in Georgia?

It varies a lot by age, health, and coverage amount, but many healthy adults in their 20s and 30s pay well under $50 a month for a solid term policy. Brannon can give you a real number for your situation in one short conversation instead of a generic average.

How much is a $30,000 life insurance policy?

A $30,000 policy is usually a final expense or small whole life policy, and premiums typically run $30 to $100+ a month depending on your age and health. Brannon compares final expense carriers the same way he does larger term policies, to find your best rate.

How does life insurance pay out after death?

The beneficiary you name on the policy files a claim directly with the insurance carrier, usually just requiring a death certificate and a claim form. Most carriers pay out within a few weeks. It goes straight to your beneficiary, not through probate, which is one of the biggest advantages life insurance has over other ways of leaving money to your family.

Does it cost more to use an independent insurance agent instead of buying direct?

No. Insurance carriers charge the same price whether you buy directly from them or through an independent agent like Brannon — the carrier pays the agent's commission out of their own overhead, not by charging you extra.
